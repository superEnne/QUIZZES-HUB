Enterprise Architecture Comprehensive Reviewer
This reviewer is designed to help you prepare by testing your knowledge on the core definitions and concepts of Enterprise Architecture. It is divided into three parts based on the lecture notes provided.
Part 1: An Overview of Enterprise Architecture (Lecture Note 1)
1. Which of the following best defines an "Enterprise"? A) An organization that only exists in the private sector. B) A single software system used to manage corporate data. C) An organization or sub-activity whose boundary is defined by commonly-held goals, processes, and resources. D) A strict set of technology standards for network infrastructure. Answer: C Explanation: According to the text, an enterprise includes whole organizations, parts of an organization, or parts of multiple organizations bounded by commonly-held goals, processes, and resources.
2. What is the definition of "Enterprise Architecture" (EA)? A) The process of coding software for enterprise-level deployment. B) The analysis and documentation of an enterprise in its current and future states from an integrated strategy, business, and technology perspective. C) A strictly technology-driven process for upgrading IT systems. D) The physical blueprint for constructing an organization's headquarters. Answer: B Explanation: EA provides coordinated, holistic views encompassing strategy, business, and technology, rather than being a strictly technology-driven or systems-level approach.
3. What does the equation "EA = S + B + T" stand for? A) Enterprise Architecture = Systems + Budgets + Timelines B) Enterprise Architecture = Strategy + Business + Technology C) Enterprise Architecture = Security + Baselines + Tools D) Enterprise Architecture = Standards + Business + Tactics Answer: B Explanation: This equation is the "sound bite" version of EA, demonstrating that EA is driven by strategic goals and business requirements, not just technology.
4. In the context of the EA management program, what is "Governance"? A) The physical servers that run the enterprise. B) The specific steps used to establish the EA program. C) The planning, decision-making, and oversight processes and groups that determine how the EA is developed and maintained. D) The documentation of the "as-is" state of the organization. Answer: C Explanation: Governance is the first core element of the EA approach and is accomplished as part of an organization's overall governance.
5. Which core element of EA identifies the specific steps to establish and maintain an EA program? A) Artifacts B) Methodology C) Framework D) Standards Answer: B Explanation: Methodology refers to the specific steps used to implement the EA approach.
6. What is the purpose of an EA "Framework"? A) It provides the physical boundaries of the office network. B) It identifies the scope of the overall architecture and the type and relationship of the various sub-architecture levels and threads. C) It stores the financial data of the enterprise. D) It acts as the legal governing body of the organization. Answer: B Explanation: The framework's scope is reflected through its geometric design and organizes the abstracted set of "views" of an enterprise.
7. In EA, what are "Artifacts"? A) Outdated technology components no longer in use. B) Physical hardware used in network infrastructure. C) The types and methods of documentation used in each sub-architecture area, such as documents, diagrams, and models. D) The employees who manage the EA repository. Answer: C Explanation: Artifacts include strategic analyses, business plans, and models of workflow, databases, systems, and networks.
8. Which core element identifies proven ways to implement parts of the overall architecture? A) Best Practices B) Standards C) Governance D) Framework Answer: A Explanation: Best Practices are proven ways to implement parts of the architecture or sub-architectures within the overarching EA context.
9. How does EA provide "Strategic Alignment"? A) By ensuring all employees work the same hours. B) By connecting goals, activities, and resources, maximizing efficiency and effectiveness. C) By eliminating all outdated technologies instantly. D) By allowing technology to drive business planning. Answer: B Explanation: Strategic Alignment connects goals to resources, helping to promote the enterprise's competitive capabilities and questioning projects that are not aligned.
10. Which management program element supports financial control and configuration management? A) Resource Oversight B) Strategic Alignment C) Standardized Policy D) Decision Support Answer: D Explanation: Decision Support provides visibility for IT initiatives at executive, management, and staff levels, supporting design, configuration, and operational decisions.
11. What is the "Current Architecture"? A) The planned capabilities of the enterprise in five years. B) The "to-be" view of the architecture. C) The baseline inventory of EA components that currently exist within the enterprise at each level of the framework (the "as-is" view). D) Only the physical hardware currently in use. Answer: C Explanation: The current architecture serves as a baseline reference for project planning, asset management, and investment decision-making.
12. What does the "Future Architecture" document? A) The history of the company's technology failures. B) Only the emerging technologies that have not yet been invented. C) The new or modified EA components needed to close an existing performance gap or support a new strategic initiative (the "to-be" view). D) The current operating state of the enterprise. Answer: C Explanation: It manages the transition by documenting what is needed to support new operational requirements or technology solutions.
13. What is the purpose of the "EA Management Plan"? A) To hire new IT staff. B) To articulate the EA program and documentation approach, and provide a sequencing plan for managing the transition to the future environment. C) To define the legal structure of the enterprise. D) To strictly monitor employee internet usage. Answer: B Explanation: The EA Management Plan describes current and future views and provides the sequencing plan to transition between them.
14. Which of the following is considered a "Thread" in EA documentation? A) Marketing B) IT-related security C) Facilities management D) Financial auditing Answer: B Explanation: EA documentation includes threads of common activity present in all levels of the framework: Security, Standards, and Skills.
15. What is a "Line of Business" (LOB)? A) A single software application. B) A distinct area of activity within the enterprise, such as manufacturing or internal administrative functions. C) The direct reporting line from staff to CEO. D) A strictly external supply chain. Answer: B Explanation: A LOB is a distinct area of activity that can involve manufacturing, services, or internal functions.
16. What defines an "Architecture Segment"? A) A part of the overall EA that documents one or more lines of business at all levels and threads, capable of existing as a stand-alone part. B) A fragment of a broken database. C) A specific time period during which the EA is active. D) A single server rack in the data center. Answer: A Explanation: To lower risk and promote phased implementation, the EA framework is divided into segments focused on distinct LOBs.
17. What is a "Vertical Component"? A) A goal, process, program, or resource that serves several lines of business. B) A hierarchical organizational chart. C) A changeable goal, process, program, or resource that serves exactly one line of business. D) A system that only operates in the cloud. Answer: C Explanation: Vertical components are dedicated to a single LOB (e.g., a specific manufacturing system).
18. What is a "Horizontal (Crosscutting) Component"? A) A component that serves only one specific department. B) A changeable goal, process, program, or resource that serves several lines of business. C) The physical floor plan of the enterprise. D) A technology that has been decommissioned. Answer: B Explanation: Examples include enterprise-wide email and administrative support systems used across multiple LOBs.
19. What is a "Reference Architecture"? A) A dictionary of business terms. B) The part of an EA that provides standards and documentation for a particular type of capability throughout the enterprise (e.g., cloud computing). C) A list of competitors' architectures. D) A textbook used to train new architects. Answer: B Explanation: A reference architecture provides standards for a specific capability utilized across the enterprise.
20. What is the main function of the "EA Repository"? A) To securely destroy old data. B) To act as a website and database that stores EA documentation and provides links to EA tools to make them useable by stakeholders. C) To automatically write code for new applications. D) To manage the company's financial accounts. Answer: B Explanation: The repository archives artifacts (documents, diagrams) to provide easy access for planning and decision-making.
21. Why is EA considered a "Meta-Discipline"? A) Because it only deals with metadata. B) Because it is purely theoretical and has no practical application. C) Because it serves as an umbrella or "meta-context" for all other management and technology best practices. D) Because it was invented by a company named Meta. Answer: C Explanation: EA is holistic and organizes all other management and technology best practices (like ITIL, Six Sigma, etc.) as the standards authority.
22. Which area is NOT typically listed as a thread present across all levels of the EA3 framework? A) Security B) Standards C) Skills D) Sales Answer: D Explanation: The three threads identified in the EA3 framework are Security, Standards, and Skills (Workforce).
23. Under the EA Management Program, what does "Resource Oversight" provide? A) A lifecycle approach to development/management. B) A way to bypass IT security. C) Financial audits of the CEO. D) Marketing strategies. Answer: A Explanation: Resource oversight uses standardized lifecycle methods to reduce the risk of failing cost, schedule, or performance parameters.
24. How does EA handle "Standards"? A) It ignores external standards to focus on internal ones. B) It relies solely on proprietary vendor solutions. C) It draws on accepted international, national, and industry standards to promote non-proprietary solutions and enhance component integration. D) It assumes standards are only relevant to the technology level. Answer: C Explanation: Standards are a core element and a crosscutting thread meant to support integration and component switch-out.
25. Which is an example of a Horizontal (Crosscutting) Component? A) A specialized robotic arm for one manufacturing line. B) An enterprise-wide email system. C) A localized sales database for a single store. D) A custom marketing strategy for a specific product. Answer: B Explanation: Because an enterprise-wide email system serves multiple/all lines of business, it is a horizontal component.
Part 2: The Structure and Culture of Enterprises (Lecture Note 2)
26. What is the definition of "Culture" in an enterprise context? A) The financial standing of the organization. B) The beliefs, customs, values, structure, normative rules, and material traits of a social organization. C) The specific software programs used by HR. D) The physical architecture of the office building. Answer: B Explanation: Culture dictates how an organization functions and is an amalgamation of the values and habits of people throughout the enterprise.
27. How is a "Stakeholder" defined in the EA program? A) Only the executives who fund the project. B) Only the IT staff who implement the technology. C) Everyone who is or will be affected by a policy, program, project, activity, or resource. D) External customers only. Answer: C Explanation: Stakeholders include executive sponsors, architects, program managers, users, and support staff who are impacted by the EA.
28. According to the Leavitt Diamond, what are the four interacting components of an organization? A) Task, Structure, People, Technology B) Time, Space, Money, Power C) Teams, Strategy, Products, Tactics D) Task, Security, Policy, Tools Answer: A Explanation: Leavitt argued that a change in any of these four components (Task, Structure, People, Technology) will affect the others and underlies organizational success.
29. In the Parsons/Thompson Model, what is the "Institutional Level"? A) Where the actual "product" of the organization is processed. B) Where mediation between the organization and the task environment occurs. C) Where the organization establishes rules and relates to the larger society to secure legitimacy, meaning, and higher-level support. D) Where independent workers form external networks. Answer: C Explanation: The Institutional Level is very open to the environment, establishes boundaries, and secures legitimization to implement goals.
30. In the Parsons/Thompson Model, what is the function of the "Managerial Level"? A) Processing the actual product of the organization. B) Mediating between the organization and the immediate task environment, administering internal affairs, and handling resources/products. C) Interacting exclusively with society for higher-level meaning. D) Writing software code for the technical level. Answer: B Explanation: At the managerial level, a dynamic of mediation occurs, managing the internal affairs and resource flow.
31. In the Parsons/Thompson Model, what characterizes the "Technical Level"? A) It is where the organization establishes its overarching rules. B) It is a highly political and unformalized area. C) It is where the actual "product" is processed, acting "rationally" and trying to seal off functions from external uncertainties. D) It strictly handles public relations. Answer: C Explanation: The technical level focuses on production (input/output) functions and protects them from outside uncertainty as much as possible.
32. What is the definition of "Change Management"? A) The automated process of updating software versions. B) The process of setting expectations and involving stakeholders in how a process/activity will change, so they have some control and are more accepting. C) Randomly altering business processes to see what works best. D) Firing employees who resist new technology. Answer: B Explanation: Managing change involves giving stakeholders control to reduce their natural resistance to alterations in their environment.
33. Why is the "Home Architecture Analogy" relevant to enterprise structure and culture? A) It proves that businesses should be run from home. B) It shows that an architect must understand the composition, preferences, and activities of the occupants (people) to produce an effective design. C) It implies that building a house is cheaper than building an EA. D) It suggests that IT infrastructure should look like a residential building. Answer: B Explanation: Insight into the "people aspect" is crucial to developing policies and plans that the enterprise will actually accept, just like designing a home for a specific family.
34. According to the text, what happens if an EA program introduces changes without giving stakeholders some level of control? A) The changes are implemented twice as fast. B) The EA program may be resisted by stakeholders. C) The stakeholders will automatically support the EA. D) The technology will fail to run. Answer: B Explanation: People are often resistant to change when they lack control. If changes are forced, stakeholders will likely resist the program.
35. Which of the following is a way to successfully manage change and increase stakeholder control? A) Keeping EA plans a secret until launch. B) Involving stakeholders in EA program establishment and regularly communicating activities. C) Excluding user input from decision-making. D) Overpromising what the EA program can accomplish. Answer: B Explanation: Mitigation strategies include involving stakeholders, effective communication, allowing input, and managing expectations.
36. Enterprise Architecture is as much about people and social interaction as it is about: A) Marketing and sales. B) Processes and resource utilization. C) Legal compliance and taxation. D) Hardware manufacturing. Answer: B Explanation: The text emphasizes that EA must balance the understanding of people/culture with processes and resources to be accurate and relevant.
37. How does the presence of an EA program affect organizational change? A) It completely stops change from occurring. B) It causes change to happen in a disjointed, independent manner. C) It coordinates change so it is driven by new strategies and business requirements, rather than just new technologies. D) It ensures change is only driven by IT departments. Answer: C Explanation: Without EA, change is disjointed. With EA, change is coordinated and aligned with strategy and business needs.
38. When matching the EA3 Cube Framework to the Parsons/Thompson Model, the "Goals & Initiatives" level generally aligns with which Parsons/Thompson level? A) Technical Level B) Managerial Level C) Institutional Level D) Independent Worker Answer: C Explanation: In the alignment model (Slide 12), the higher-level Goals & Initiatives align with the Institutional Level, where meaning and higher-level support are established.
39. In the Organizational Network Model, what connects the Executive Team to the Functional Teams? A) Physical network cables. B) Lines of communication and organizational structure within the Organizational Boundary. C) External suppliers. D) The Technical Level only. Answer: B Explanation: The model shows the Executive team interacting dynamically with Functional Teams and Independent Workers inside (and sometimes outside) the boundary.
40. Why do enterprise cultures differ even within the same organization (sub-enterprises)? A) Because they use different computers. B) Because culture is an amalgamation of the distinct values, beliefs, habits, and preferences of all people in that specific area. C) Because IT policies enforce different cultures. D) Because stakeholders do not exist in sub-enterprises. Answer: B Explanation: Every enterprise and sub-enterprise is unique because of the distinct amalgamation of the human elements (values, habits) within them.
41. In the Leavitt Diamond, if the "Technology" component is changed, what is the expected result? A) No other components will be affected. B) Only the "Structure" will change. C) The Task, Structure, and People components will also be affected. D) The enterprise will automatically fail. Answer: C Explanation: Leavitt argued that a change in any of the four components has an effect on all the others.
42. Which field is considered a contributing field to the "Organizational Theory" influence on EA? A) Computer Science B) Engineering C) Sociology D) Information Security Answer: C Explanation: Organizational theory draws from Psychology, Sociology, Political Science, and Public Administration.
43. Which field is considered a contributing field to the "Systems Theory" influence on EA? A) Psychology B) Operations Research C) Political Science D) Sociology Answer: B Explanation: Systems theory draws from Engineering, Computer Science, Business Administration, and Operations Research.
44. What defines an "Organization" versus an "Enterprise" in the context of the EA3 framework alignment? A) Organizations only involve technology. B) Enterprises are types of social organizations where concepts of organizational theory are applicable. C) Organizations have no stakeholders. D) Enterprises do not have a culture. Answer: B Explanation: Enterprises are defined as types of social organizations, meaning organizational theory concepts directly apply to the practice of EA.
45. Which of the following is NOT one of the contributing concepts from Organizational Theory to EA? A) Beliefs B) Culture C) Systems Lifecycle Development D) Bureaucracy Answer: C Explanation: Systems Lifecycle Development is an emerging concept, whereas Beliefs, Culture, and Bureaucracy are foundational organizational theory concepts.
46. In the Parsons/Thompson model, which level tries to "seal off" its functions from external uncertainties? A) Institutional B) Managerial C) Technical D) Executive Answer: C Explanation: The Technical Level acts rationally to carry out production and tries to seal itself off from outside uncertainty.
47. Managing stakeholder expectations involves: A) Telling them the EA will fix every problem immediately. B) Ensuring they understand realistically what the EA program can and cannot do. C) Forcing them to read the entire EA framework manual. D) Ignoring their concerns to maintain project speed. Answer: B Explanation: Managing change successfully includes setting proper expectations about the capabilities of the EA program.
48. What is the ultimate risk of ignoring the culture and structure of an enterprise when building an EA? A) The EA software will crash. B) The EA will not accurately reflect organizational goals or gain stakeholder support. C) The EA will be completed too quickly. D) The enterprise will be forced to change its name. Answer: B Explanation: Understanding structure and culture is necessary to accurately reflect true processes and manage expectations to gain stakeholder support.
49. An EA program acts as a bridge between different stakeholders, such as: A) Only internal employees. B) Business units, IT teams, and executives. C) Only the CEO and the Chief Architect. D) Only external customers and vendors. Answer: B Explanation: The EA helps break down silos and aligns business and IT objectives across various stakeholder groups.
50. What is a key characteristic of the Managerial level in organizational structure? A) It strictly deals with raw material input. B) It is less formalized and more political as it mediates between the organization and the task environment. C) It establishes the highest level of societal rules. D) It is completely isolated from all external forces. Answer: B Explanation: The managerial level mediates between the institutional and technical levels, dealing with internal affairs in a more dynamic, political environment.
Part 3: The Value and Risk of Creating an Enterprise Architecture (Lecture Note 3)
51. What is the primary overall value of Enterprise Architecture? A) It guarantees an immediate increase in stock price. B) It enhances resource-planning capabilities and supports better decision-making. C) It eliminates the need for human workers. D) It allows the enterprise to operate without a budget. Answer: B Explanation: EA brings together views of strategy, business, and technology to help the enterprise see itself, enhancing planning and decision-making.
52. Which of the following is considered a Financial Risk associated with implementing an EA? A) The EA tools are too difficult to use. B) Stakeholders refuse to participate in the EA process. C) The significant cost of establishing current/future views, plans, and regular updates. D) The enterprise's strategic goals are too vague. Answer: C Explanation: The financial risk relates to the cost and time it takes to hire architects, buy tools, take staff away from normal work, and maintain the repository.
53. How does EA enhance "Top-down planning"? A) By starting with technology upgrades first. B) By beginning with considerations for strategy and business, enhanced by holistic perspectives of the enterprise. C) By eliminating the need for executive input. D) By allowing individual programs to plan independently. Answer: B Explanation: Top-down planning starts with strategy and business, which EA supports through its holistic, enterprise-wide perspective.
54. How does EA enhance "Bottom-up planning"? A) It coordinates what would otherwise be disparate and separate program-level planning activities. B) It forces lower-level employees to create the strategic vision. C) It ignores LOB requirements in favor of executive mandates. D) It mandates the use of legacy systems. Answer: A Explanation: EA provides a baseline reference that coordinates and aligns lower-level, separate program activities with the larger enterprise.
55. Which of the following is a risk defined as "Loss of Key Personnel"? A) Losing executives who do not understand EA. B) The departure of skilled architects, analysts, and developers, creating delays and affecting implementation costs. C) Firing employees who resist change. D) The retirement of the company founder. Answer: B Explanation: EA requires specific skill sets. Losing members of the EA team can cause significant program delays and cost overruns.
56. What is the risk associated with "Lack of Acceptance"? A) The EA tools are incompatible with current hardware. B) The EA represents a new way of looking at resources that stakeholders may reject, preventing the realization of value. C) The budget is not approved by the finance department. D) The strategic goals are not accepted by the public. Answer: B Explanation: If stakeholders do not buy into the concept of EA or its perceived value, they will resist it, rendering the EA useless.
57. What is "Risk Mitigation" in the context of an EA program? A) Ignoring uncertainties until they become problems. B) Actions that lower uncertainty, such as strengthening executive support, ensuring trained backups, and using detailed methodologies. C) Eliminating all technology from the enterprise to prevent cyber attacks. D) Only addressing financial costs. Answer: B Explanation: Mitigation plans reduce the likelihood that sources of risk will negatively impact the program (lowering uncertainty).
58. How does an EA help quantify value through "Shortening Planning Cycles"? A) By skipping the planning phase entirely. B) By providing a robust repository of on-line information regarding current and future processes, reducing time spent gathering data separately. C) By limiting the number of people allowed in planning meetings. D) By outsourcing planning to a third party. Answer: B Explanation: Access to integrated, baseline information means planners don't have to start from scratch, significantly shortening the cycle.
59. How does EA contribute to the "Reduction of Duplicative Resources"? A) By firing half of the IT staff. B) By aiding the visualization of where current resource value areas overlap, allowing for the elimination of redundant data or systems. C) By buying more servers to handle data overload. D) By isolating business units so they cannot share resources. Answer: B Explanation: Visualizing the architecture reveals duplicative resources (e.g., overlapping databases), representing "low-hanging fruit" for cost savings.
60. What is meant by quantifying value through "Reduced Re-work"? A) Never updating software applications. B) Avoiding conflicting program-level initiatives and misunderstandings through holistic planning and standard modeling techniques. C) Lowering the quality standards of final products. D) Outsourcing all development work. Answer: B Explanation: Holistic planning and a common language reduce misunderstandings and conflicting projects, which directly reduces the need to re-do work.
61. How does EA quantify value through "Fewer People in a Process"? A) By arbitrarily firing employees to meet budget cuts. B) By supporting Business Process Reengineering (BPR) to eliminate repetitive parts of a process, equating to payroll savings. C) By replacing all staff with Artificial Intelligence. D) By making employees work longer hours. Answer: B Explanation: Streamlined processes that use resources more efficiently require fewer manual steps, leading to position reductions/payroll savings.
62. When quantifying EA program costs, which approach is recommended? A) Looking only at the initial purchase price of EA software tools. B) Approaching it from a program lifecycle view (implementation, maintenance, and refreshment phases). C) Calculating only the salaries of the IT department. D) Ignoring indirect costs completely. Answer: B Explanation: Costs should be viewed over the lifecycle, estimating direct and indirect costs for start-up, ongoing management, and documentation updates.
63. In linking EA and Strategy, what are "Strategic Goals"? A) The business and technology activities that execute the mission. B) The primary objectives of the enterprise, typically requiring several years to accomplish. C) The outcome metrics that identify when a project is finished. D) The daily operational targets of individual workers. Answer: B Explanation: Strategic goals are long-term, primary objectives altered in response to internal/external drivers.
64. In linking EA and Strategy, what are "Strategic Initiatives"? A) Outcome measures that define when the enterprise "wins." B) The primary objectives requiring several years to accomplish. C) The business and technology activities, programs, and projects that enable the accomplishment of strategic goals. D) The software applications used by the HR department. Answer: C Explanation: Initiatives are the actionable programs/projects that enact the fundamental direction to achieve the goals.
65. In linking EA and Strategy, what are "Strategic Measures"? A) The physical dimensions of the enterprise's facilities. B) Outcome measures that identify when a strategic initiative has successfully met a strategic goal. C) The budget allocated to the EA program. D) The number of employees in a line of business. Answer: B Explanation: Measures are the outcomes that define when the enterprise is accomplishing its mission (when it 'wins').
66. When linking EA and Business Planning, why is it important to document business activities? A) To justify increasing executive bonuses. B) To support Business Process Improvement (BPI) and identify inputs, outputs, outcomes, and links between processes. C) To eliminate the need for technology entirely. D) To find reasons to terminate employees. Answer: B Explanation: Documenting the creation and delivery of products/services supports BPI/BPR and reveals how processes interact.
67. When linking EA and Technology Planning, what is the primary role of technology? A) To act as the sole catalyst for organizational change. B) To dictate the strategic goals of the enterprise. C) To serve as a resource that enables information and resource flows to support business products/services, which achieve strategic goals. D) To consume as much of the budget as possible. Answer: C Explanation: Technology is an enabler, supporting solutions for business requirements; it should generally not drive business/strategic planning.
68. Why is "bottom-up planning" (technology driving change) considered risky for resource-constrained enterprises? A) Because technology changes too slowly. B) Because the expense of duplicative, non-strategic technologies cannot be afforded. C) Because it requires hiring too many executives. D) Because strategy is irrelevant in small enterprises. Answer: B Explanation: While viable in some cases, bottom-up planning can result in expensive, non-strategic duplication, which is damaging to constrained budgets.
69. Which of the following is considered a direct cost of an EA program? A) The general electricity bill of the entire company. B) Salary/benefits for a Chief Architect and EA team staff. C) The marketing budget for the enterprise's core product. D) The cost of raw materials for manufacturing. Answer: B Explanation: EA team salaries are a direct lifecycle cost of implementing and maintaining the EA program.
70. What is a key risk related to "Documentation Tools" in EA? A) They are too cheap and lack basic functionality. B) They are difficult to use for producing intuitive and informative "management views" of detailed EA information. C) They are illegal to use in most countries. D) They automatically delete old data. Answer: B Explanation: A great challenge for a Chief Architect is that current EA tools often struggle to easily produce intuitive management views from rich detail.
71. How does EA improve communication? A) By creating a common language and standardized approaches across the organization. B) By forcing everyone to learn advanced programming languages. C) By eliminating the need for face-to-face meetings. D) By keeping information restricted to the executive team. Answer: A Explanation: EA brings standardized terminologies and integrated information, acting as a bridge between different stakeholders.
72. Does EA replace strategic planning or business process improvement? A) Yes, EA completely replaces all other forms of planning. B) Yes, EA makes BPI obsolete. C) No, EA does not replace them, but enhances them by contributing useful, integrated information. D) No, EA has no relationship to strategic planning. Answer: C Explanation: While EA shortens planning cycles, it does not replace these activities; it augments them.
73. What role does an alternatives analysis, cost-benefit analysis, and ROI calculation play in an EA program? A) They are used to punish failed projects. B) They are the primary measures for evaluating the EA program's contribution to profitability/mission success (building a business case). C) They are only used when closing a business down. D) They are strictly required for IT security purposes. Answer: B Explanation: These are essential to building the business case to quantify EA value for executives and LOB managers.
74. How does EA help with "More Effective Planning Meetings"? A) By providing catered food for the attendees. B) By allowing meetings to be conducted entirely via email. C) By presenting a common baseline of reference information, reducing ambiguity. D) By eliminating the need to have an agenda. Answer: C Explanation: EA reduces ambiguity and increases common understanding by ensuring everyone is looking at the same integrated baseline data.
75. What is a key step in identifying supporting technologies through EA? A) Buying the most expensive software available. B) Analyzing business requirements and activities to reveal critical dependencies (e.g., marketing needs sales data, manufacturing needs robotics). C) Waiting for vendors to pitch their products. D) Copying the exact technology stack of a competitor. Answer: B Explanation: EA helps identify and document supporting technologies directly tied to analyzing what the business requirements actually need.
